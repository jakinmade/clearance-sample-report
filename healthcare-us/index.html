This is what a US health plan receives after a CLEARANCE diagnostic of an AI system that is already live. The example is a regional health plan using an AI tool that reads clinical notes and recommends approve, pend or deny on prior authorisation requests, in Colorado, Texas and California. PHI goes to an external AI API under a signed BAA, and data is encrypted, but there is no clinician review before an AI-influenced denial, no audit trail of what the AI accessed, and no named owner. The findings are built from HIPAA and the state laws now regulating AI in utilisation review. A US financial services sample and a US insurance sample are also available.

High risk

Nothing here blocks deployment outright, but seven high-severity gaps need closing within 90 days. The most exposed: AI-influenced prior authorisation denials reaching members with no clinician review first, and no log of what patient data the AI used.

What was found

Each gap fires only when specific answers are present, and the report shows which ones. For each, the report states what it looks like when it goes wrong and the first thing to do about it.

SeverityGap and what it looks like in practiceFirst action
HIGHNo decision boundary enforcementAn incident investigation asks a simple question: what governed this decision before it executed? For this system, there is no answer. The output became the action with nothing in between.Introduce a rule evaluation layer between this system's output and the action it takes. Even a minimal documented ruleset is a material improvement on none.
MEDIUMProvenance weaknessA regulator or auditor asks where the data came from that drove a specific decision. Tracing it back takes days, not minutes, and for some inputs there is no trail at all.Create a register of every data source feeding this system. Assign an owner and a validation status to each.
HIGHAudit voidSomeone affected by a decision disputes it and asks to see the record. The decision happened. The record of it did not.Start logging every decision this system makes today: date, input, output, rule applied, in a write-once store. This is the foundation. Every other governance control depends on it existing.
HIGHNo documented legal basis for AI processing of protected health informationIf OCR audits your AI deployment, the first question is: what is your legal basis for processing this PHI? Without documented HIPAA authorisation or a recognised exception, there is no answer.Document the HIPAA legal basis for every AI use of PHI this week. Assign a named owner. Review with legal counsel within 30 days.
MEDIUMPHI used by AI beyond its original collection purpose without state law compliancePHI collected for one purpose, say, billing, is being processed by AI for another. Without state law compliance, that is a HIPAA purpose limitation violation.Map every AI input to its original collection purpose. Flag any processing that goes beyond that purpose. Check state law requirements for your operating states.
MEDIUMNo retention policy for AI-processed patient dataAI systems create new categories of patient data records. Without a retention policy covering AI-processed PHI, you are accumulating liability with no defined endpoint.Extend your existing data retention policy to cover AI-processed PHI specifically. Define retention periods by PHI type. Confirm with your privacy officer.
HIGHNo process for patient rights in AI-influenced healthcare decisionsPatients have HIPAA rights over data used in decisions about them. If AI influences those decisions and patients have no mechanism to exercise those rights, that is a structural gap, not a policy gap.Define and document the process for patients to request access to, or correction of, AI-influenced decision records. Disclose AI use in patient-facing communications.
HIGHAI used in coverage decisions, prior authorisation, or utilisation review without required human oversightA denied patient appeals. Their lawyer asks for the decision record. The denial was generated by an AI, and no clinician ever reviewed it.Map every AI system touching coverage decisions today. Document the human review step. Where it doesn't exist, implement it before the next denial cycle.
HIGHNo audit logging for AI access to or use of PHIDuring an OCR investigation, nobody can produce logs showing what patient data the AI accessed, when, and why.Implement audit logging at the AI inference layer before the next deployment cycle.
HIGHNo incident response or breach notification process covering AI-related PHI exposureSixty-one days after an AI-related PHI breach, OCR asks for the incident response documentation. The plan never covered AI in the first place.Update your incident response plan before the next AI deployment cycle.
